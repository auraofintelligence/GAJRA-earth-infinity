# Voice and Tone

## Audience

Write to a new or seasoned explorer mapping infinity. They may build AI, question it, live beside it or move between those positions. Assume curiosity, agency and the ability to choose a heading.

## Voice

- Offer a field guide, workbench or observatory rather than a manifesto.
- Use questions, coordinates, possible paths and visible trade-offs.
- Prefer `you can explore`, `one possible map` and `choose what travels` over commands.
- Keep disagreement and changes of mind available.
- Explain technical terms without talking down to the visitor.

## Boundaries without a boss

Factual status, provenance, consent and privacy still need plain labels. Describe what exists, what happened, what a tool transmits and what a contributor selected. Do not turn those labels into a nanny, gatekeeper or universal decree.

Use `must`, `should`, `never` and similar language only where a legal, safety or technical fact genuinely requires it. The Strange But True licence remains a separate legal boundary.

## Self-sovereign default

The visitor chooses what to define, store, export, share, dispute, revise or leave unfinished. The site supplies instruments and maps; it does not appoint itself ruler of the territory.
